Any block with a photo box lets you either upload a photo from your computer or phone, or paste a link to a photo that’s already online.

Add a photo

  • Upload: click ⬆ Upload and pick the file. DoorStep shrinks it to a sensible size for phones and saves it in the page.
  • Link: paste the photo’s web address into the box and press Enter. On most websites you can right-click a photo and choose “Copy image address”.

Boxes that take several photos (Hero, Photo grid) show each one as a small thumbnail with ‹ › to reorder and ✕ to remove.

Keep pages quick

People open your page on phone data, often in a building with weak signal. A handful of uploaded photos is fine. For lots of photos, link to them instead of uploading, so the page itself stays small.

Upload quality

Uploaded photos are resized so their longest side is 1,400 pixels, as good-quality JPEGs. To change that, open the Publish tab → Uploaded image quality. It applies to photos you upload from then on.

Shape and fit

  • Image shape crops the photo to a shape: Wide 16:9, Photo 3:2, Square, Portrait 4:5, Tall 9:16 and more. Original size keeps the photo as it is.
  • Image fit (on a Hero): Fill crops the edges to fill the shape, Show whole image fits it all in.
  • On a Spotlight, use Zoom, Left / right and Up / down to frame the part of the photo you want.

Make a slideshow

Add two or more photos to a Hero block and it fades between them. Set Seconds per image. For photos people swipe through themselves, with captions, use a Slides block instead.

Add video

You wantUse
A sermon or YouTube/Vimeo videoA Video block. Paste the video’s normal link.
A short, silent moving backgroundA Spotlight block set to A video. Upload a clip under 6 MB, or paste a link to an .mp4 file. It plays muted on a loop, like a moving photo.

Bulletins, flyers and PDFs

To show a whole bulletin or flyer that people can read and zoom, don’t put it in a photo block. Use a button set to Show a photo or PDF. See Photo and PDF viewer.

Describe your photos

The Image block has a Description box. Write what the photo shows, like “Families talking in the lobby after church”, so people using screen readers know what’s there.
